# The Question Is Not Whether You Are Christian
## The Question Is Whether You Are Following Jesus

### A Come-to-Jesus Meeting About Yeshua, Christianity, and Christian Nationalism

There is a difference between **being Christian** and **following Jesus**.

That is not an insult.

Christianity is a religion nearly two thousand years old. It contains Yeshua—Jesus—but it also contains Paul, other New Testament authors, bishops, councils, creeds, theologians, churches, denominations, traditions, governments, political movements, and centuries of interpretation.

Many Christians sincerely try to follow Yeshua.

But the words are not synonyms.

A person can call themselves Christian because they believe Christian doctrine, attend a Christian church, were raised Christian, accept a creed, read the Bible, or simply identify with the religion.

Following Yeshua is a narrower claim.

It raises a harder question:

**Are you actually following what he taught?**

That question becomes especially important when his name is used to determine what may be done **to somebody else**.

If you are going to restrict another person's freedom, control their family, interfere with their medical decisions, punish their sexuality, subordinate them to a spouse or parent, exclude them because of religion, remove their rights, deport them, impoverish them, or use government power against them—and you say you are doing it because of Jesus—then Yeshua himself becomes relevant evidence.

Not merely Christianity.

Not merely the Bible.

Not merely your denomination.

Not merely your pastor.

**Yeshua.**

This is not an attempt to decide who is a “real Christian.”

Christian nationalism does not automatically make someone non-Christian. Progressive Christianity does not automatically make someone a faithful follower of Yeshua. Religious identity and faithfulness to a teacher are separate questions.

This article asks only the second.

**If we claim Yeshua's authority for what we do to other people, did Yeshua actually teach us to do it?**

---

# First, We Have to Find Yeshua

There is a problem.

We do not possess a transcript of Yeshua speaking.

We do not possess anything he wrote.

We do not even possess the original handwritten copies of the Gospels.

What survives are manuscript relatives—copies descended from earlier copies—which scholars compare to reconstruct the earliest recoverable Greek text. Critical Greek editions exist because manuscripts disagree and textual history has to be reconstructed rather than assumed.

And Yeshua himself probably taught primarily in Aramaic within a multilingual Jewish environment, while the surviving Gospels were composed in Greek.

So a modern English Bible is several layers downstream:

**Yeshua and the earliest traditions  
→ Greek Gospel composition  
→ manuscript transmission  
→ reconstructed Greek text  
→ modern translation  
→ modern interpretation**

That does not make historical recovery impossible.

It means humility is required.

Mark appears to preserve our earliest surviving Gospel narrative. Matthew and Luke frequently use Mark while also preserving other material. John is later and considerably more explicitly theological in form.

When those witnesses differ, the differences matter.

When a manuscript has a later addition, it matters.

When an English word hides ambiguity in Greek, that matters.

When Matthew appears to alter Mark, that matters.

And when Yeshua says nothing at all about a modern issue, **that matters too**.

This article therefore does not claim to reproduce Yeshua's exact syllables.

It asks a more defensible question:

**What can we recover with reasonably high confidence about the direction of his teaching?**

---

# Core Yeshua

The Yeshua that survives the filtering process is neither a modern progressive mascot nor a modern conservative one.

He is a first-century Jewish religious teacher with an apocalyptic worldview.

He believes in one God.

He believes God's Kingdom is coming.

He expects repentance.

He believes wrongdoing is real.

He believes in resurrection.

He expects judgment.

He can be demanding and severe.

His conception of God remains hierarchical in a way many modern people may reject.

We should leave all of that intact.

But something else survives just as strongly.

His human moral teaching repeatedly pushes **against domination**.

He centers love of God and neighbor.

He demands mercy even toward enemies.

He repeatedly places responsibility on people with power.

He warns about wealth.

He challenges religious authorities.

He makes leadership into service.

He refuses to make biological family morally absolute.

He treats relationships as creating obligations rather than ownership.

He allows people to reject him.

And when his own followers become punitive, exclusionary, status-seeking, or controlling, they are frequently the people he rebukes.

That is our starting point.

---

# Christianity Is Not Automatically Yeshua

This distinction needs to remain visible throughout everything that follows.

A teaching can be genuinely Christian without being something Yeshua personally taught.

Christian communities began developing theology almost immediately.

That is not necessarily sinister.

Religions develop.

People encounter new situations and reason from inherited teachings.

An early Christian prohibition on abortion, for example, appears in the *Didache*. That is historically important evidence about early **Christianity**.

But the *Didache* is not Yeshua.

Paul is enormously important to Christianity.

Paul is not Yeshua.

A church council can define Christian orthodoxy.

The council is not Yeshua.

A pastor can make a theological argument based on the Bible.

The argument may even be reasonable.

But it is still not automatically something Yeshua said.

We therefore need five different sentences:

**Yeshua taught this.**

**An early Christian taught this.**

**A later Christian theologian concluded this.**

**My denomination teaches this.**

**My political movement wants this.**

Those are not interchangeable statements.

If Christianity develops beyond Yeshua, fine.

Just call the development what it is.

The laundering begins when something developed elsewhere is put back into Yeshua's mouth.

---

# Yeshua and Political Power

This is where Christian nationalism creates an immediate problem.

In April 2026, 17% of American adults told Pew that the federal government should declare Christianity the country's official religion. Another 43% said government should not formally establish Christianity but **should promote Christian moral values**. Among Protestants, those numbers were 26% and 52%; among Catholics, 15% and 52%.

PRRI's 2025 American Values Atlas classified about one-third of Americans as Christian-nationalist Adherents or Sympathizers. Among white evangelical Protestants, the figure was 67%.

So this is not an imaginary debate.

Millions of Americans believe Christianity should enjoy some degree of governmental preference.

Now look for Yeshua's instruction to do that.

It is remarkably difficult to find.

When Yeshua describes ordinary rulers, he says they **lord power over people**.

Then he tells his followers:

**Not so among you.**

Greatness becomes service.

Being first becomes serving others.

That is not a blueprint for modern democracy.

Yeshua ultimately expects **God** to rule.

But neither is it a blueprint for Christian dominion over one's neighbors.

There is no surviving Yeshua instruction resembling:

**Take control of the government.**

**Establish my religion as the preferred religion of the state.**

**Make outsiders obey our religious law.**

**Remove equal standing from people who reject us.**

**Use Caesar's machinery to make people follow me.**

Those propositions are simply not found.

Someone may derive them from later Christian theology.

But then that is where the argument comes from.

Not from a command of Yeshua.

---

# Judgment Is Not a License to Rule Other People

Yeshua believed in judgment.

We should not erase that.

But a belief that divine judgment will occur is not the same proposition as:

**My followers should obtain governmental power and perform that judgment themselves.**

Yeshua evaluates conduct. He rebukes people. He warns people. His God is not morally indifferent.

But look at what his followers are actually instructed to do when somebody refuses them.

They leave.

They may warn the person.

They may regard the rejection as serious.

But they leave.

And one narrative makes the distinction unusually vivid.

A Samaritan village refuses Yeshua.

James and John want destructive punishment brought down upon it.

Yeshua rebukes **James and John**.

Then they go elsewhere.

Some later manuscripts elaborate the rebuke with additional language about destroying and saving lives. That extra wording should not be treated as secure Yeshua.

We do not need it.

The older narrative already says enough:

**They refused him.**

**His followers wanted them punished.**

**He rebuked his followers.**

**They left.**

Whatever Yeshua believes God will eventually do, he does not thereby tell his followers:

**Become God's police.**

That distinction is critical.

---

# What If Someone Simply Does Not Follow Him?

Mark preserves another tiny episode that deserves far more attention than it receives.

The disciples encounter somebody acting in Yeshua's name who does not belong to their group.

Their complaint is essentially:

**He doesn't follow us.**

Yeshua tells them not to stop him.

Notice the complaint.

Not:

**He is hurting somebody.**

Not:

**He is exploiting somebody.**

Not:

**He is committing violence.**

He is simply **not one of us**.

That is not sufficient reason to suppress him.

That matters enormously whenever religious belonging is treated as a prerequisite for equal participation in society.

Yeshua does not seem especially impressed by the argument:

**But they're not part of our group.**

---

# Who Does Yeshua Rebuke?

Some of the strongest patterns become visible when we ask not only what Yeshua condemns but **where he directs his confrontational energy**.

His own followers get rebuked.

Peter gets rebuked.

Followers trying to exclude children get corrected.

Followers trying to suppress outsiders get corrected.

Followers proposing retaliation get corrected.

Religious authorities receive severe criticism when authority becomes status, hypocrisy, exploitation, or a way to place burdens on other people.

In Mark, Yeshua condemns religious leaders who seek honor while consuming widows' resources.

He attacks religious loopholes that allow someone to perform religion while evading real obligations toward another person.

His criticism therefore frequently moves **upward and inward**.

That does not mean powerless people can do no wrong.

It means claimed authority seems to **increase accountability rather than decrease it**.

That creates a difficult implication for modern religious politics:

**The more loudly someone claims Yeshua's authority over everybody else, the more—not less—their use of power should be tested against Yeshua.**

---

# Abortion and Forced Pregnancy

Here the historical answer starts with three words:

**Yeshua is silent.**

There is no surviving direct Yeshua teaching on abortion.

That does not prove he approved of abortion.

It does not prove he opposed it.

It means we do not have his teaching.

Later Christianity develops an explicit anti-abortion tradition early. That should be acknowledged rather than hidden.

But later Christian teaching is still later Christian teaching.

It cannot be converted into:

**Yeshua explicitly said this.**

And that becomes especially important as the argument moves from personal morality into coercion.

There is no surviving teaching in which Yeshua says:

**A woman impregnated through rape must be forced to remain pregnant.**

There is no surviving teaching prescribing punishment for a woman who has an abortion.

There is no surviving teaching directing the state to regulate pregnancy according to his religion.

There is no surviving teaching telling physicians to sacrifice a woman's health until a pregnancy reaches some religiously acceptable threshold of danger.

Again, none of this proves what Yeshua would conclude if someone could transport him into a modern obstetric ward and explain abortion medicine to him.

We cannot conduct that experiment.

But we can refuse to pretend that we already did.

And there is a moral pattern that must remain in the room when Christians reason about the question.

Yeshua repeatedly prioritizes mercy.

He heals.

He challenges hard-hearted enforcement of religious rules.

He asks what religious obligation is **for**.

And violence, domination, and exploitation are things he actually gives us substantial material to evaluate.

So a Christian is free to construct a later moral argument about abortion.

But if that argument produces suffering for another human being, **“Jesus says so” is not enough**.

Show where.

---

# Sex, Lust and Adultery

This may be one of the clearest examples of later interpretation accumulating around Yeshua.

Yeshua clearly has sexual morality.

He condemns sexual wrongdoing.

He condemns adultery.

He treats marital bonds seriously.

But that does not mean every sexual rule later associated with Christianity came directly from him.

Mark's divorce saying is particularly revealing.

The Greek says that a man who dismisses his wife and marries another commits adultery **against her**:

*moichatai ep' autēn.*

The relational object matters.

Someone has been wronged.

This does not justify expanding the verse into every kind of modern romantic relationship.

The secure point is narrower:

**You cannot simply discard an existing spouse, replace her, and pretend no betrayal occurred merely because you used the available legal mechanism.**

That is remarkably relational.

Matthew gives us the famous saying about looking at a woman with *epithymeō*.

That word can mean desire or coveting. The construction involves looking **in order to desire/covet**, rather than merely noticing that another person is attractive.

So:

**involuntary attraction ≠ automatically adultery.**

The teaching concerns what someone does with desire.

And we should remember that Matthew's formulation is not preserved in the same form by Mark and Luke.

It may preserve Yeshua.

It may also preserve Matthew's development of Yeshua tradition.

The distinction stays visible.

---

# Did Yeshua Say You Need Government Permission Before Having Sex?

No surviving saying does.

First-century Jewish marriage could involve genuine legal and social structures: betrothal, witnesses, contracts, families, obligations, and community recognition.

So ancient marriage was not merely:

**Two people privately feel married.**

But neither was it the modern American system of a government-issued marriage license.

When Yeshua speaks of what **God has joined**, he is making a theological and relational claim.

He is not discussing county paperwork.

A civil marriage can exist between two atheists who believe no god joined them at all.

That legal marriage is real **as a civil status**.

But government recognition and sacred relationship are clearly not identical concepts.

So we should distinguish:

**civil marriage**

**religious marriage**

**relational commitment**

They can overlap.

They are not automatically the same thing.

Yeshua's surviving sexual teaching therefore gives us fidelity, betrayal, sexual wrongdoing, and serious relational obligations.

It does **not** give us a complete twenty-first-century governmental sex code.

---

# Gay and Transgender People

The same evidentiary discipline applies here.

There is no surviving Yeshua teaching directly addressing transgender identity.

There is no surviving Yeshua saying directly discussing a modern same-sex romantic relationship.

Yeshua invokes male-and-female creation language in a debate about **divorce**.

Christians can reason outward from that passage and develop a broader theology of marriage.

Many have.

But the reasoning outward is **the reasoning**.

It is not a new quotation from Yeshua.

Going from:

**Yeshua invoked Genesis while opposing divorce**

to:

**therefore the state should deny this modern group this particular civil right**

requires several additional propositions.

Those propositions have to be defended.

“Jesus” cannot simply be inserted where the bridge should be.

A Christian may conclude that something is sinful.

But another proposition follows:

**What did Yeshua teach his followers to do to people they believed were wrong?**

Those are separate questions.

---

# Family, Parents, Spouses and Abuse

Yeshua affirms honoring parents.

But he does not make biological family morally sovereign.

Mark's own narrative makes this difficult to miss.

Yeshua's family attempts to intervene in his activity.

Later in the same episode he redefines family around people doing God's will.

Other traditions preserve even more severe sayings about family loyalties losing priority.

Whatever else those sayings mean, they make one interpretation extremely difficult:

**Your biological relatives have permanent moral authority over you merely because they are your relatives.**

They do not.

Honor therefore cannot simply mean:

**Obey regardless of what they do.**

Yeshua himself does not behave that way.

Likewise, his teaching against abandoning a spouse cannot responsibly be transformed into:

**An abused spouse must remain available to an abuser.**

Those are not the same proposition.

Condemning someone who discards a spouse in pursuit of another person does not imply that the abused party has surrendered the right to protect themselves.

Relationships create obligations.

They do not turn one person into the property of another.

Forgiveness does not require pretending wrongdoing did not happen.

Love does not require unlimited access.

Honor does not require submission to abuse.

Yeshua can call people to reconciliation while still rebuking wrongdoing.

Those ideas can coexist.

---

# Wealth: The Teaching That Becomes Strangely Quiet

If modern Christianity's political emphasis were reconstructed solely from some American culture-war debates, an observer might conclude that Yeshua spent enormous amounts of time discussing other people's sexual behavior and relatively little time worrying about wealth.

The surviving material gives almost the opposite impression.

Yeshua talks about wealth repeatedly.

And often harshly.

A wealthy man asks what is required.

Yeshua gives him a devastating answer: relinquish his possessions, give to the poor, and follow.

The man cannot do it.

Mark says Yeshua **loved him**.

And then lets him leave.

That little encounter captures several themes at once.

Yeshua can love someone while disagreeing with them.

He can demand something radical.

The other person can refuse.

Yeshua lets him walk away.

And afterward he warns how dangerous wealth can be.

There is no serious historical route from this to the prosperity gospel idea that wealth is straightforward evidence of divine favor.

Nor does it resemble an ethic where accumulating enormous wealth is treated as morally neutral while poverty becomes primarily the fault of the poor.

Yeshua is not a modern socialist economist.

He provides no national budget.

But suspicion of wealth, concern for the poor, generosity, debt, status reversal, and economic obligation run deeply through his teaching.

Anyone claiming to construct politics from Yeshua cannot simply make that portion disappear.

---

# Immigrants and Outsiders

Yeshua did not give us an American immigration code.

He does not tell us what today's legal immigration quotas should be.

He does not define asylum procedure.

Again, silence should remain silence.

But Christian nationalism often reaches beyond technical immigration policy into ideas about outsiders as threats to the nation's identity.

Yeshua does not give us a border statute capable of settling that debate.

But he gives us a moral grammar.

Enemies remain objects of moral concern.

Outsiders are not automatically enemies.

People outside the disciples' group are not automatically to be stopped.

Someone's refusal to follow Yeshua does not authorize his disciples to destroy them.

Those principles do not answer every immigration question.

They do make dehumanization difficult to reconcile with him.

---

# Enemies and Violence

Yeshua's enemy teaching is inconveniently direct.

Love your enemies.

Do good toward people who hate you.

That is not sentimental niceness.

It is a demand concerning precisely the people for whom ordinary moral reciprocity has broken down.

Yeshua still believes in judgment.

He still distinguishes right from wrong.

But he does not seem to accept the idea that calling someone evil erases one's obligations toward that person's humanity.

And again, when his own followers want to answer rejection with destruction, **the followers get rebuked**.

That should matter whenever Christianity is used to cultivate pleasure in cruelty toward political, religious, ethnic, sexual, or national enemies.

A person can believe someone is profoundly wrong without becoming morally authorized to do anything whatsoever to them.

Yeshua repeatedly preserves that distinction.

---

# Religious Rules Exist for Something

“The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath.”

That sentence may contain one of Yeshua's most consequential interpretive principles.

A rule has a purpose.

Human beings are not merely raw material to be sacrificed so that the rule can remain perfectly untouched.

Yeshua does not thereby abolish religious law.

He interprets it through purpose, human need, mercy, and the character of God as he understands God.

That principle should haunt every religious policy that produces obvious human suffering.

The question cannot stop at:

**Can I derive a rule?**

It has to continue:

**What is this rule doing to the human being standing in front of me?**

That is an unmistakably Yeshua-shaped question.

---

# Following Yeshua Is Harder Than Claiming Him

This is where the distinction between **Christian** and **follower of Yeshua** becomes useful.

Calling yourself Christian is relatively easy.

Following Yeshua is extremely inconvenient.

Following Yeshua means that when gaining power would allow you to dominate others, his warning about domination still applies.

When an enemy frightens or disgusts you, enemy-love still applies.

When someone refuses you, their refusal does not become permission to force them.

When wealth benefits you, his teachings about wealth do not disappear.

When religious authority benefits you, his attacks on religious hypocrisy do not become somebody else's problem.

When your family behaves wrongly, blood does not magically make wrong right.

When your own group tries to suppress somebody merely because the person is not “one of us,” Yeshua's correction still applies.

When you want God to punish somebody and would very much like to assist, the Samaritan village still exists in the record.

Christian identity does not remove any of those tensions.

If anything, claiming Yeshua should make them harder to evade.

---

# None of This Makes Yeshua Ours

This article is not recruiting him into another ideology.

Yeshua may disagree with us about God, judgment, sexual morality, or the structure of reality.

Fine.

Let him disagree.

Historical honesty requires it.

Yeshua believes things modern leftists may dislike.

He believes things modern conservatives may dislike.

He belongs to first-century Judaism, not to either American political party.

The point is not that Yeshua secretly agrees with us about everything.

The point is much simpler:

**Other people do not get to make him secretly agree with them either.**

If we discover something he taught that undermines this article's argument, it stays.

If we discover a saying was added later, it goes.

If the Greek is ambiguous, it remains ambiguous.

If Yeshua is silent, we say he is silent.

That is the only way to avoid using him exactly as we are accusing other people of using him.

---

# Christianity Can Go Beyond Yeshua

There is nothing historically strange about Christianity developing teachings that Yeshua never personally articulated.

Every living tradition develops.

Christians may believe the Holy Spirit continued guiding that development.

Catholics may recognize church authority.

Protestants may construct doctrine from the broader biblical canon.

Orthodox Christians may appeal to apostolic tradition.

Those are theological claims Christians can debate among themselves.

But something changes when those later developments are imposed on people who do not share them.

Then the distinction becomes unavoidable.

You are no longer merely saying:

**My religion teaches me to live this way.**

You are saying:

**My religion gives me authority to make you live this way too.**

That second proposition requires far more justification.

And invoking Yeshua does not supply it unless Yeshua actually authorized it.

---

# The Burden of Proof

So here is the standard I am proposing.

Before using Yeshua's name to justify doing something **to another human being**, ask:

### Did Yeshua actually teach this?

If yes, show it.

### Is the passage textually secure?

If not, say so.

### Is this Yeshua speaking, or is it the Gospel author's explanation?

Keep those separate.

### Does the Greek actually require the English interpretation being claimed?

If not, preserve the ambiguity.

### Is this teaching from Yeshua, or from later Christianity?

Name the actual source.

### Is this a moral belief or a permission to coerce?

Do not silently jump from one to the other.

### If Yeshua believed someone was wrong, what did he actually tell his followers to do about that person?

That may be the most important question of all.

Because:

**“Yeshua believed this was wrong”**

does not automatically mean:

**“Yeshua told me to control everyone who does it.”**

Those are different claims.

---

# A Come-to-Jesus Meeting

I am not asking Christians to abandon Christianity.

I am not declaring who is or is not a Christian.

I am not asking anyone to become politically left, right, liberal, socialist, conservative, or anything else.

I am asking for something more elementary.

**Give Yeshua the dignity of allowing him to have his own beliefs.**

If Paul says something Yeshua never said, call it Paul.

If an early church says something Yeshua never said, call it early Christianity.

If Augustine says it, call it Augustine.

If a denomination says it, name the denomination.

If a politician says it, put the politician's name on it.

If **you** believe it, own your belief.

There is nothing wrong with making an argument.

There is something wrong with putting the argument into the mouth of a dead man who cannot correct you.

And there is something especially dangerous about doing that when the invented quotation becomes justification for controlling, punishing, excluding, impoverishing, or hurting somebody else.

The historical Yeshua that remains after two thousand years of transmission is still perfectly capable of making people uncomfortable without our help.

He believes in judgment.

He demands repentance.

He challenges wealth.

He attacks hypocrisy.

He undermines domination.

He demands service from leaders.

He refuses to make family sovereign.

He tells followers not to suppress outsiders merely because they are outsiders.

He tells them to love enemies.

He allows people to refuse him.

And some of his hardest criticism is directed at people claiming religious authority while failing the people beneath it.

So perhaps the most important question facing political Christianity is not:

**Are you a Christian?**

Anybody can claim a name.

The question is:

# Are you following Jesus?

And if your answer involves gaining power over somebody else, Yeshua deserves the opportunity to answer too.
